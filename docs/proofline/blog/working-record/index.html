Find the evidence gap before drafting a confident response

Proofline editorial · 14 November 2026 · 4 min read

A drafted answer is not an assurance opinion or certification. A qualified reviewer decides whether the evidence is sufficient.

Start with the limits of the evidence.

A single supplier review can involve dozens of long documents and hundreds of questions. The AI must find the relevant passage, compare it with the question and identify conflicting or absent evidence.

The drafted response explains the scope gap and cites both documents. The reviewer decides whether the answer is sufficient.

Make the review practical.

  1. Keep the exact buyer question beside the proposed answer.
  2. Check whether the policy covers the system, people and period being assessed.
  3. An absent passage means unanswered, not automatically non-compliant.
  4. Have a qualified reviewer accept the answer before it is sent outside the team.

One concrete example.

A questionnaire says that every user has multi-factor authentication; the policy only mentions administrator accounts.

The prepared review cites both passages and drafts a request to clarify the missing scope.

Ask whether the policy and enforcement also cover standard user accounts.

Keep the decision with the responsible person.

A risk reviewer accepts the answer or requests more evidence. A drafted answer is not an assurance opinion or certification. A qualified reviewer decides whether the evidence is sufficient.
