What makes a maintenance work order useful

Brickwise editorial · 14 November 2026 · 4 min read

A useful work order record helps the next person understand the situation without reconstructing the entire conversation.

Start with the operating context.

A practical maintenance board for commercial property teams. Turn a fault report into a named work order, a clear priority and a recorded resolution. The goal is to make a small number of important facts easy to find: the record reference, the location, the person responsible and the unresolved question.

A priority, a technician and the context to get it done. Open a work order to see the report. A table only becomes a workflow when someone can tell what needs attention and what the next step should be.

Give every open question an owner.

An open question needs a named person and a practical follow-up. A sample priority score can help sort a queue, but it cannot replace the judgment of the team responsible for the work.

Get building faults out of the group chat and into a work order.

A short checklist for the handover.

  1. Use a stable reference for each work order.
  2. Record the relevant location and operational context.
  3. Explain the outstanding question in plain language.
  4. Name the person who will take the next step.
  5. Record the outcome before closing the item.

Keep the register useful.

Review open work orders regularly. A record that no one updates becomes another place to look, rather than a shared account of the work. Make the review part of a real team rhythm and keep the fields short enough for people to maintain.

The Brickwise demonstration lets you try that sequence with illustrative records. Search the register, open a detail panel, add a note and record the next action. The demo data is not a report of actual customer operations.
