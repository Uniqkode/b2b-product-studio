Check whether the document covers the actual job

Gatehouse editorial · 14 November 2026 · 4 min read

AI reads contractor documents and flags missing or expired evidence for a reviewer.

Start with the input the team already has.

Large sites onboard many contractors, each with several documents that expire or change. The AI reads the file, extracts the relevant dates and compares the evidence with the job requirements.

For industrial workplaces and facilities teams. Gatehouse compares training certificates, insurance schedules and work-permit requirements, then explains what is missing before a contractor starts a job.

Make the review practical.

  1. Compare the document’s scope with the work the contractor is being asked to do.
  2. Check the planned job dates against the insurance period.
  3. A supplied certificate is not the same as an authenticated certificate.
  4. Record the appointed reviewer’s decision; software should not silently grant access.

One concrete example.

The supplied insurance ends on 15 November, while the planned job starts on 18 November.

The prepared checklist cites the policy date and requests evidence covering the work period.

Ask the contractor for current evidence covering the planned maintenance period.

Keep the decision with the responsible person.

An appointed workplace manager checks the source and makes the decision. It checks supplied documents. It does not authenticate a certificate, identify a person or independently authorize workplace access.

The product’s sample workspace lets you follow that chain: source, finding, review and retained action. The example is prepared for demonstration and does not claim measured customer performance.
