Where weight variances enter a grain ledger

GrainLedger editorial · 14 November 2026 · 4 min read

A useful lot record helps the next person understand the situation without reconstructing the entire conversation.

Start with the operating context.

A working grain register for aggregation teams. Reconcile intake, quality and dispatch before a small discrepancy becomes a costly one. The goal is to make a small number of important facts easy to find: the record reference, the location, the person responsible and the unresolved question.

Weight, quality and exceptions, on the same line. Open a lot to review the intake note. A table only becomes a workflow when someone can tell what needs attention and what the next step should be.

Give every open question an owner.

An open question needs a named person and a practical follow-up. A sample priority score can help sort a queue, but it cannot replace the judgment of the team responsible for the work.

Bring every grain lot from weighbridge to dispatch into one ledger.

A short checklist for the handover.

  1. Use a stable reference for each lot.
  2. Record the relevant location and operational context.
  3. Explain the outstanding question in plain language.
  4. Name the person who will take the next step.
  5. Record the outcome before closing the item.

Keep the register useful.

Review open lots regularly. A record that no one updates becomes another place to look, rather than a shared account of the work. Make the review part of a real team rhythm and keep the fields short enough for people to maintain.

The GrainLedger demonstration lets you try that sequence with illustrative records. Search the register, open a detail panel, add a note and record the next action. The demo data is not a report of actual customer operations.
