Why photos and scale tickets belong in the same intake check

GrainLedger editorial · 14 November 2026 · 4 min read

Visual grading is a screening step. A warehouse specialist confirms the grade, sampling method and test results.

Start with the limits of the evidence.

Every truck creates new photos and paperwork. The AI has to inspect many grains in each image, read the related tickets and compare the results before it can flag a lot for review.

Hold the release decision until the quality observation and weight difference have been checked.

Make the review practical.

  1. Standardize the light, tray and camera distance before comparing grain samples.
  2. Keep dispatch and receipt weights separate until the difference is explained.
  3. A visual flag should lead to a physical quality check.
  4. Retain the source ticket beside the final lot decision.

One concrete example.

A 30.0-tonne dispatch ticket and a 29.4-tonne receipt ticket are stored in separate delivery folders.

The proposed AI reads both tickets and places the 0.6-tonne difference beside the lot-quality check.

Take a representative physical sample and confirm the grading test.

Keep the decision with the responsible person.

A quality manager reviews the evidence and records the decision. Visual grading is a screening step. A warehouse specialist confirms the grade, sampling method and test results.

The product’s sample workspace lets you follow that chain: source, finding, review and retained action. The example is prepared for demonstration and does not claim measured customer performance.
