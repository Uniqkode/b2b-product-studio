What a soil moisture reading can tell you

Fieldwell editorial · 14 November 2026 · 4 min read

A useful field record helps the next person understand the situation without reconstructing the entire conversation.

Start with the operating context.

Turn field observations into a practical inspection plan. Built for agronomists managing multi-site grower operations. The goal is to make a small number of important facts easy to find: the record reference, the location, the person responsible and the unresolved question.

A short list for a long day. Review moisture, crop stage and the last inspection in one place. A table only becomes a workflow when someone can tell what needs attention and what the next step should be.

Write for the next person.

Write the observation in concrete terms. Describe what was seen, what was checked and what is still uncertain. Avoid a conclusion that the available evidence cannot support.

See which fields need attention before your next farm visit.

A short checklist for the handover.

  1. Use a stable reference for each field.
  2. Record the relevant location and operational context.
  3. Explain the outstanding question in plain language.
  4. Name the person who will take the next step.
  5. Record the outcome before closing the item.

Keep the register useful.

Review open fields regularly. A record that no one updates becomes another place to look, rather than a shared account of the work. Make the review part of a real team rhythm and keep the fields short enough for people to maintain.

The Fieldwell demonstration lets you try that sequence with illustrative records. Search the register, open a detail panel, add a note and record the next action. The demo data is not a report of actual customer operations.
