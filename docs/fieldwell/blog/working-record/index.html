What a crop photo can—and cannot—tell an agronomist

Fieldwell editorial · 14 November 2026 · 4 min read

It supports scouting. A photograph alone cannot confirm disease, nutrient deficiency or a treatment.

Start with the limits of the evidence.

One drone survey can contain hundreds of overlapping images. The useful job is to compare new observations with the last survey and group repeated signs of stress into areas a field team can inspect.

Two areas need a field visit. The report links each observation to the source; an agronomist chooses the response.

Make the review practical.

  1. Keep the survey date and field boundary with every image.
  2. Compare the same part of a field across dates; shadows and camera position can create false changes.
  3. Group repeated observations before dispatching a team.
  4. Ask an agronomist to confirm the cause before choosing a treatment.

One concrete example.

An agronomist receives a 240-photo survey and must find the same affected area across overlapping images.

The proposed AI groups the evidence into two inspection areas, each linked to its source photos.

Inspect Area C4 before deciding whether this is a water, nutrient or disease issue.

Keep the decision with the responsible person.

An agronomist reviews the evidence and assigns an inspection. It supports scouting. A photograph alone cannot confirm disease, nutrient deficiency or a treatment.
